# Evidence: attribution over-decline re-check, and joint-event telling verbs (v1.17, 2026-10-03)

**What changed.**
- **Attribution re-check.** When an ordinary judgment declines a contribution because no one spoke (manufactured attribution), the engine now asks the judge once more, using a separate tool. The second call names the contribution's ground: a directive or publication rendered to this call, a named outside party's publishing act, a telling event the contributor was in, or the contributor's own first-hand claim about its own scope. The engine then checks that answer against the contribution's text.
  - The decline is reversed only when every check passes, and the result is context at most, never a directive.
  - Any other decline ground still stands, including contradiction with an inherited directive.
  - Any failure, or an answer the engine can't read, leaves the first decline unchanged.
- **Joint-event telling verbs (#225 widened).** "Discussed with X", "agreed with X", "decided with X", "met with X" and "in our sync with X" now count as telling events in both the #225 interior-assertion check and the re-check. This follows the philosopher's ruling of 2026-10-03: a conversation the contributor took part in places it as party or audience.
  - A bare "as discussed, …" names nobody and still fails.
  - Hearsay from a joint verb is recorded at that verb's strength ("In discussion with X: …"), never as "X says".

**How it was measured.** Numbers here come from the strata-evals file bridge, with Claude (Sonnet) answering each judge request in turn. They are directional, and not comparable with the pinned-qwen numbers in other evidence files. The pinned-qwen numbers for the release tree are in the v1.17 baseline evidence. Build: `feat/v17-attribution-recheck` at `ac750fd`.

Held-out sets were labelled blind by the philosopher: `j1_attribution` (48 items) and `j4_joint_verb` (18 items) in strata-evals.

**Forged-answer test of the checks themselves.** No judge is involved. For every decline-golden item in j1_attribution, j4_interior, j4_joint_verb and the J4 adversarial set (128 items), the most favourable forged re-check answer was built for each ground, with every other decline ground marked clear. With scope names as production writes them, **6 items** still pass a forgery. All 6 are the alias limit below.

Two rulings by the philosopher shaped the checks:
- A rescue must ground **every** claim the contribution attributes to its source. A publication padded with a requirement it never stated stays declined, and the reason names the unsourced part.
- Conflicting with the scope's **own** directive is not a decline ground for this re-check. Contradicting an inherited one is.

## Stated limits

- **Aliases and collective nouns aren't matched.** "The purchasing team" for a scope named procurement isn't recognised as another scope. This is the same limit as #225.
- **Mechanical proxies.** Several checks are word-list proxies: engine-directed vocabulary, unnamed third parties ("another team", "review board"), and a second requirement clause joined by "and"/"also". Padding without a requirement verb can slip the last one, and the judge's "other grounds clear" answer is the backstop.
- **Conduct toward the contributor isn't a re-check ground.** A conduct observation that names another scope ("procurement-ops's agent turned down our ask") isn't rescued by this path, and stays declined if the first call declined it. #225's first-call conduct check is where that shape is admitted.
- **Recorded form not shown on the bridge.** That joint-verb hearsay is recorded in the "In discussion with X" form is covered by unit tests. The bridge traces don't carry the written context.
- **Single-contribution path only.** As with #225, batched judgments aren't covered.
