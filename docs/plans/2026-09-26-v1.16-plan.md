## Lessons from v1.15, applied here

These lessons are rules for every item below.

1. **Never put a verdict in an optional judge field.** The measured judge
   (qwen3-235b-a22b-2507) reliably fills `decision` and nothing optional:
   - a sidecar `outcome_disposition` field scored 23/47; moving the verdict into `decision` scored 44/47;
   - `withdraw_published` was omitted every time;
   - #209's `changed_circumstance` and M1's attestation fields were never filled.

   A verdict goes into `decision`. An invariant the theory states is enforced by the engine
   (as with P4 decision A), never hung on a field the judge may omit.
2. **A design note comes before any code** for every engine change. It runs to about 12 lines and
   says who is affected, which record facts change, and what the judge sees. The architect approves
   it first. This caught two over-broad designs in v1.15, and each catch was cheaper than a revision.
3. **The no-regression proof is input identity.** Before a change touches a judge input, capture
   fixtures of the rendered prompt and every tool schema from the base, in a commit of their own.
   Then pin that every path the change does not target is byte-identical. J1/J4 run as noise
   context: every per-item difference is reported in both directions, and any failure is re-run
   on the pre-change build. They are not a pass/fail criterion.
4. **Every merged tree also runs the strata-evals mechanical suite** (docs/releasing.md item 12).
   It is the only check that calls the judge interface from outside the repo.
5. **Report a result the moment it lands.** Attribution follows in a separate message.
6. **Worked examples in judge prompts leak into reasons.** In v1.16 the judge quoted the
   acted_on block's own example ("used port 8443, the service refused") as the contributor's words,
   in 14 of 20 reasons. The ADR 0016 conduct example measured 10/10 on the item worded like it and
   8/10 on a reworded twin. A judge prompt carries placeholders, not concrete worked examples. No eval
   item may share a domain, a name or a phrasing with a prompt example (the strata-evals README
   rule).
7. **Per-item n=10 does not gate a stochastic judge.** The same item on the same build scored 10/10,
   10/10 and then 8/10, and a sibling went from 10/10 to 5/10. Gates are pooled over a class (for
   example 60 reps before and 60 after), reported as a rate with both counts. Every gate report
   records the OpenRouter provider that served the judge: runs are routed across providers (fp8 and
   first-party), and that is a variance source in its own right.
8. **Unpinned measurements cannot gate prompt wording.** On the conduct/interior boundary, the
   same base prompt misread conduct 13–25% of the time depending on the hour's provider mix. A
   wording fix that measured 25%→10% in one session failed in the next (22% vs 13%). The
   base already admits flatly stated interior assertions (j4-833: 20/20; #225).
   **Freeze (CEO, 2026-09-26):** no further prompt-text work on this boundary until the eval key
   is provider-pinned.
9. **Evidence before claims.** Every number a public claim uses lives in `docs/evidence/` before
   the claim is made, and each wording claim names the test that pins it.

Every item has a 3-revision bound. The philosopher checks the contract lines of P5–P7 before
any code, as was done for ADR 0017.

### 3. P5 — directive consequences go upward

Today, a failed outcome on a directive is accepted as context and goes nowhere. P5 makes the
**engine**, not a judge field, raise it to the scope that **issued** the directive:
- as an ordinary upward contribution, judged there with the reporter's provenance;
- or, for an operator directive, into the operator's view.

It is bounded to once per outcome. The directive is never weighed down (Concept 6). Nothing
binding erodes quietly; the issuer revises through the ordinary channel, or doesn't.

Eval: ol-010's routing half becomes scored, and a new item covers an operator directive.

**P5 decisions (philosopher-checked; CEO-approved 2026-09-26):**
- **Contract change to P3, directive targets only:** the acting judge's decision is
  {held, failed, decline}, never failed_corrected/failed_superseded. It does not own the directive;
  the issuer classifies. This needs its own gate with the input-identity proof.
- **The engine raises every `failed` undifferentiated**, and only when issuer ≠ reporter:
  - to a scope issuer, as an ordinary upward contribution with the reporter's provenance,
    `acted_on` = the directive, `raised_from` = the outcome;
  - for an operator directive, as an `operator_evidence` row, unjudged and verbatim. It is shown
    with the directive in the Console operator view, and `seen_at` is set only by the operator's act.
- **held is never raised.** A raised contribution is never re-raised; the mechanism stops at the
  issuer, whose own judgment may escalate.
- The reporter's side shows "raised to <issuer> as <id>", derived from `raised_from`.
- The wording is always "following <directive> went wrong".
- Migration 0018 has **two** new columns, not one: `contributions.raised_from`, and
  `contributions.acted_on_operator_item` (→ `operator_acts`), with a CHECK that at most one of it and
  `acted_on` is set, plus the `operator_evidence` table. The reason: P1's `acted_on` is an FK to
  `contributions`, and operator directives never enter that table (ADR 0008 D4). The API keeps one
  `acted_on` field, and the app resolves which column the id belongs in. *Note to P1's "one column"
  line (ADR 0017 plan): it held for context and scope-directive targets only.*
- A raised contribution is judged by a synchronous second judge call in the same request; a failure
  retries through the ordinary rejudge path. It counts in write-back and session stats as
  **system-raised**, never as the reporter's.

### 4. P6 — decay by condensation order

1. **Engine side, first.** The #202 condensation signal records, for each dropped item, whether
   it was corroborated, corrected or unexamined at the time. The order becomes visible in the record.
2. **Judge side.** The condensation rewrite prompt lists each carried item's corroboration, and
   says to drop **unexamined** before **corroborated**. This deliberately changes the condensation
   input, so the proof for that path is a new eval: an over-budget scope where unexamined context
   is dropped before corroborated context, at 10 reps. Identity is pinned for every other path.

The wording is "unexamined", never "poorly standing".

**P6 decisions (CEO-approved 2026-09-26):**
- **Part 1, engine only:** at the #202 stamping site, every accepted context item that is present
  verbatim before a write and absent after it gets a `condensation_drops` row (migration 0019):
  scope, summary version, contribution, `state_at_drop` ∈ {corroborated, correcting, raised, unexamined} (derived, not stored; a raised P5 consequence is examined, because standing follows the item's ground, not its location), the summary
  word count before and after, and the budget. The row is shown in the record, the Console and
  `strata record`. It merges on its own.
- **Part 2, judge input:** when a scope holds at least one corroborated context item still present,
  the rewrite input gains a "CORROBORATED CONTEXT" block (the item verbatim, with its count of held
  outcomes) and one instruction line: drop unexamined context before corroborated context. The
  block is absent otherwise, so identity holds everywhere else.
  - Gate: an over-budget class, 3 domain variants × 20 reps = 60 per side, run in the same
    session with providers recorded.
  - **Pass:** the unexamined-first rate rises by **at least 15 points** pooled, and nothing else
    regresses.
  - Run pinned if the key is pinned by then. If it isn't, and the rise is under the floor, part 2
    is parked like 1c, with the numbers.
**Philosopher's check (2026-09-26), all seven lines kept, with these additions:**
- The correcting content of a failed outcome counts as **examined**, on the same footing as
  corroborated context. Its ground is an outcome, and it is the scope's only defence against the
  retracted claim returning as an echo. Part 2's instruction therefore reads "drop unexamined before
  examined", and one gate variant pits a correcting item against an unexamined one.
- **Corroboration is never a lock.** It is evidence of past use and reliability, not of present
  relevance. A scope's purpose moves, and decay is about relevance (Concept 7).
- **Any held outcome counts for the decay order,** self-corroboration included. Independence of
  source is read only at P7 (ratification).
- The stated limit: condensation is event-driven, so this is the trust half and the usage half of
  decay without the time half.

**P6 part 1 finding (2026-09-26):** in live runs the judge condenses by paraphrasing, so
"absent verbatim" is the common case, not the edge case. Drop rows are therefore worded "no longer
verbatim in the summary at vN (condensed away or reworded)", never "condensed away" alone. #202's
condensation count has had the same over-approximation since 1.13; a line goes in its docs and in
the README's condensation sentence (release docs pass). #227 tracks a mechanical split between
reworded and absent, for display only. Part 2's gate reads per-item **fact markers**, not
verbatim presence.

### 5. P7 — ratification reads standing

The ratification prompt shows each candidate's **derived** standing: the corroborating outcomes,
their reporters' scopes, and whether they are independent of the item's source. The hazard items
gate it:
- a restatement is not corroboration;
- reasons that embellish evidence (the ol-007 pattern);
- one scope's standing is not reach (5b / ol-009 becomes executable).

This is the largest judge-side risk, so it comes last. **If P5/P6 use up the bound, P7 moves to
v1.17.** The CEO accepted that in advance, and the architect says so as early as it is known.
